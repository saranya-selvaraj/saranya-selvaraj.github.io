Product Analysis

Yuka: Closing the Trust Gap in Health Scores

Independent product analysis · Interactive prototype in Google AI Studio

Overview

Yuka is a mobile app that scans a food or cosmetic product and returns a health score based on nutritional quality, additives, and other factors. This was an independent, three-phase product analysis of the live app: a structured teardown of the current experience, primary user research to find the problem that mattered most, and a prioritised solution taken through to an interactive prototype.

The Problem

Health-conscious users had stopped trusting the score. Products they knew were fine for them, like a toasted seed snack that is naturally high in fat, came back "poor"; ultra-processed cereals came back "excellent". The score is generic by design: it doesn't account for how processed a food is, a person's goals, or portion size, so people either did their own research and ignored it or drifted away. In practice they had narrowed the app down to an additive checker, which meant no reason to upgrade and a slow leak in retention.

"I don't understand why some healthy products are scored so low just because it has a bit of salt."

Approach

I worked through it in three phases.

  • Phase 1: Teardown. Mapped every flow in the app end to end (onboarding, scan and score, recommendations, and the return visit), tagging each step as a smooth or friction moment. Ran a competitive scan against Open Food Facts, Think Dirty, Nutrasafe, and the NHS Eatwell Guide, split the user base into five segments, and consolidated the findings into one problem list, each item carrying a confidence level and source.
  • Phase 2: Research. Ran 5 user interviews, a 54-response survey, and a synthesis of 150+ reviews from Reddit, Trustpilot, and the App Store. Built two personas, Susan and Conor, and tested the Phase 1 hypotheses against the evidence, confirming some and dropping others (weekly insights and rewards, for instance, turned out not to matter). Scored the surviving problems on persona impact, frequency, and severity to land on a single P0.
  • Phase 3: Solution. Brainstormed six solutions against the root causes: oversimplified scoring, disregarding individual needs, and missing detail on factors like food processing. RICE-scored them and took two forward as P0: a food-processing indicator on the scan result screen, and a lightweight goal profile that reinterprets Yuka's existing score through the user's priorities, surfaced as a "what this means to you" panel. Sequenced the food-processing indicator first (higher RICE, lower effort, reaches everyone) with the profile as phase 2, gated on a privacy review and one open UI question. Built an interactive prototype in Google AI Studio, then wrote the PRD: user stories, P0 to P2 requirements with acceptance criteria, success metrics, a risks-and-mitigations table, and the open questions blocking design.

Prioritisation

Solution RICE Decision
Food-processing indicator on the scan result screen 13.5 P0, ships first
Goal profile plus a "what this means to you" reading of the score 9 P0, phase 2 after food processing
Pull health metrics from connected devices Moonshot Deferred, niche and integration-heavy
Rework the core scoring algorithm 4 Deferred, personalisation reinterprets the score instead
Ingredient-hazard dashboard 6 Deferred, low impact and overwhelm risk

Prototype

The prototype shows the same product, a tomato-sauce bean tin scoring 84/100, read three ways: with no profile set, against a weight-loss goal, and against a muscle-building goal. Each personalised view adds a plain-language "what this means to you" panel alongside the food-processing indicator. It walks through the phase-2 personalisation end to end; in the delivery plan the food-processing indicator ships on its own first.

Outcome / Impact

Turned an open-ended "analyse this app" brief into a prioritised, phased bet backed by primary research: make the score transparent and contextual by reinterpreting it, not rebuilding it. The food-processing indicator ships first; the goal profile follows once a privacy review of health-profile data (Yuka operates in the EU) and an open "clear verdict versus explanation only" UI question are resolved. Success is measured on profile completion and personalised-score engagement as leading indicators and quarterly free-tier retention as the key metric, with a holdout group so personalisation's effect can be told apart from the food-processing change shipping alongside it.

What I Learned

The research changed my mind. Going in, I assumed the gap was re-engagement features like weekly insights; the interviews and survey said people already came back often enough, and the real damage was to trust in the core score. That pushed me toward the lower-risk option: rebuilding the scoring algorithm was the moonshot, but reinterpreting the existing score against a user's goals addressed most of the frustration for far less effort. Scoping it properly surfaced what the first pass missed, that health-profile data is sensitive under EU rules and needs a privacy review before any onboarding is built, and that shipping personalisation next to the food-processing change means planning a holdout group up front so the two effects can be told apart.
